2026-11-23
**Your Pawn walks with you**

On the map, your lead Pawn now follows you around.

In the clip, we're on Saltmere's quay at dusk. The Pawn trails one tile behind the player and steps along the same path, east, south and back again.

Also in:
- The Team screen shows a "Follows you" tag on the Pawn that walks behind you. Reorder the party and the tag moves with it.
- A Follower Pawn on/off switch in Settings > Gameplay, for anyone who prefers an empty road behind them. It's on by default.

Still early, and we're not done with it. Tell us in #feedback how it feels.
2026-11-19
**Making battles hit harder**

This round was about how fights *feel*. Here's a short self-playing 3v3 on the 3D moor.

In the clip, every hit now has weight:
- The struck Pawn flashes white, then into the attacker's element colour
- The fight freezes for a beat on impact
- The screen shakes and a shockwave ripples out
- The damage number pops

Also changed: in battle, your Pawns now always face their opponents. Your side sits lower left, seen from behind, and the foes sit upper right, facing you.

We're working on the defence side too (the sliding bar, Brace and Riposte parries) and will show that next.

**First music loops for Pawnbound**

Pawnbound has had sound effects and cries for a while. Now it has the first four music loops too.

In the clip they play one after another, with their names on screen:
- **Title**: organ and choir
- **Saltmere**: strings and a bell
- **Route 1**: a reedy flute

The mood is the same as the look of the game: old, lonely and a bit damp, with a few warm notes breaking through the gloom.

These are first loops, not the finished soundtrack. They'll change as we hear them next to the places they belong to.

Headphones recommended.
2026-11-05
**The look of Pawnbound: pixel-art monsters in small 3D worlds**

Here's a first look at the visual style we're building towards: dark HD-2D. Pixel-art characters and monsters stand as sprites inside small 3D scenes with real lighting, soft shadows and depth.

In the clip:
- A small test plot built from our 3D tile kit: floors, walls, roofs and water
- Pixel-art walkers and monsters standing in it, lit like they belong there
- The same scene under each of our dark lighting presets

We're also building low-poly props for Saltmere and Route 1 (a beached rowboat, pier planks, crates) to fill these scenes with, seen at dusk and by moonlight.

The mood we're after: cold, muted colours, heavy shadow and fog, with a few warm lights breaking through. It's still early and the test plot is a long way from a finished town, but this is the direction.

Monsters stay 2D pixel art, and the arenas they fight in are built the same 3D way.
2026-10-29
**Our game has a name: Pawnbound**

Until now we've been calling it "Monster Card RPG", which was always a placeholder. From today it's **Pawnbound**.

What stays the same:
- 2D pixel-art monster collecting with a dark, moody feel
- Turn-based battles where timing matters
- Everything you carry is a card: monsters, seals, healing and more

It's still early and we're building the foundations, but the game finally has a name to go with the work. The clip shows the title screen as it looks in our current build.

If you've been following along in #devlog, thanks for being here from the start. Tell us what you think of the name in #feedback!
2026-10-29
**Catching a wild monster: the seal throw**

Capturing now works on the real battle screen, and here's the first look.

In the clip:
- A seal card is picked from the Cards menu
- It arcs across the field toward the wild monster
- The monster breaks free on the first try, so the capture isn't a sure thing

Capturing is built around the fight itself: the idea is that a weakened monster is easier to catch, and the throw has its own timing. It's still early, and the look and feel will keep changing.

What would you want to see when a capture succeeds? Tell us in #feedback.
2026-10-29
**First look: the 3v3 battle screen**

Here's a self-playing 3v3 wild battle on our new battle screen, still early and very much in progress.

In the clip:
- Monsters slide in onto their platforms
- Each one gets an info pill with an HP bar that drains as the hits land
- Three monsters a side, all on one screen

Three-a-side fights are where we want timed strikes, parries, dodges and team combos to all come together, so getting this screen readable comes first.

We're also working on how art gets into the game, so more monsters can show up on these platforms over time.

Anything you'd want to see on a battle screen? Tell us in #feedback.
